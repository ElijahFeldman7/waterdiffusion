Helicopters and turboprop airplanes operate on different principles, which is why they have different requirements for stability and control.

### Helicopters:
Helicopters are designed to hover, fly, and land vertically. They rely on a main rotor to generate lift and thrust. The main rotor is due to the weight of the helicopter and the lift generated by the blades. When the main rotor is spinning, it creates a torque that can cause the helicopter to spin if not counteracted.

To counteract this torque, helicopters use tail rotors. These tail rotors are mounted at the rear of the helicopter and rotate in the opposite direction to the main rotor. This creates a torque that balances the torque generated by the main rotor, preventing the helicopter from spinning.

### Turboprop Airplanes:
Turboprop airplanes, on the other hand, are designed to fly in the air. They use a propeller to generate thrust, which pushes the aircraft forward. The propeller is driven by a turboprop engine, which is a type of small jet engine.

Turboprop airplanes do not have a main rotor like helicopters. Instead, they rely on the propeller to generate lift and thrust. The propeller's blades are angled to create lift, which allows the aircraft to stay in the air and move forward.

Turboprop airplanes also have control surfaces, such as the wings, elevators, and rudder, which allow the pilot to control the aircraft's pitch, roll, and yaw. Additionally, turboprop airplanes are equipped with navigation systems, such as GPS and inertial instruments, which help the pilot maintain a steady course and avoid spinning in the air.

In summary, while helicopters have a main rotor that can generate lift and potentially spin, they use tail rotors to counteract the torque and maintain stability. In contrast, turboprop airplanes rely on their propellers, control surfaces, and navigation systems to achieve stable and controlled flight.
